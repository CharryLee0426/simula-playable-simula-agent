How it plays: a turn-by-turn brain, and reflexes when the game is too fast

The planner decides on a frozen frame, so it can take as long as it needs. For runners and dodgers one decision per screenshot is still too coarse in game time, so the planner hands a policy to a faster loop and takes control back whenever something new appears.

default Turn-by-turn play

One planner call per turn. The game is frozen from step 1 to step 4.

1
Observe

Screenshot, numbered interactive DOM elements when there are any, the input events the game listens to, the player’s own running notes and its last six actions.

2
Decide: planner ‖ decider, on the same frame

Planner returns screen, phase, progress, notes, 1–4 gestures and how long to watch. In parallel the decider answers: which screen phase, did the last input work (before / after images), would a first-timer know what to do.

3
Validate

Code checks the plan (types, coordinates 0–1000, element numbers, durations). Problems go back to the model to fix; after three failures a generic probe is used.

4
Humanize

Lognormal reaction time, extra reading time after a big screen change, Fitts-law movement time, curved Bézier paths with jitter, presses that last a few frames, the occasional slip.

5
Act

Touch events through the browser’s input layer, one frame at a time, so canvas, DOM and WebGL games receive the same stream a finger would produce.

6
Watch

Step 0.3–2.5 s of game time, then back to step 1.

StuckThree turns without visible effect: the planner is told to try another place or gesture.
End cardHold it for 2.5 s, tap the call to action once, stop.
Limits90 s of game time, 60 planner calls, $2 per session.

escalation Reflex policy

The planner writes it once; the decider runs it. Chosen by the planner only when things reach the player between screenshots.

A
The policy

Where the player is, an attention zone just ahead of it, and 2–5 situations, each with its gesture: “a pink block directly ahead and the left lane is empty → swipe left”.

B
Every 0.1 s of game time

Track the player by template matching, move the zone with it, crop, and ask the decider one multi-option question: which situation is happening now?

C
Fire like a person

When an action clearly beats “nothing needs a reaction”, wait a reflex delay of about 210 ms, fire, then commit to that move for 0.4 s. A move that does not move the tracked player has hit a wall and is suppressed for 1.5 s.

D
Every 0.5 s: look at the whole screen

Is play interrupted? Is there something the policy does not cover? Was the player just hit? Any yes ends the policy.

E
Learn from a hit

A flash or a sudden freeze in the recorded frames, confirmed on the whole screen. Reaction fired under 0.5 s before the hit: seen too late, the zone looks 15 % further ahead. Over 1.2 s: too early, it looks less far. In between: the situations were wrong, and the planner is told.

Hand-back. A crash, a popup, a new obstacle type or a new tutorial prompt returns control to the planner, which sees the new thing while the game is frozen and writes a better policy.
